# Gear progression playbook

Use from Balance Modeler or Crash Test when complete loadouts, replacement and repeated raids determine the answer. Read the current gear, weapon, protection, battery, weight, entry, loss and economy owners. Do not carry historical loadout assumptions into a revised rule.

Compare relevant complete kits: cheapest replaceable, intended baseline, expensive protection with cheap lethality, strong weapon with minimal protection, an older specialist kit, a squad sharing one expensive tool, and the practical upper end. Each is a test hypothesis, not mandatory authored content.

Track acquisition/replacement cost, actual loss exposure, task coverage, repeatable kill pressure, survival, tempo/resource use, mobility/loot capacity, signature risk and time to recover. Separate entry eligibility from combat capability and economic value. Do not collapse these into gear score.

Test whether cheap weapons retain all practical lethality without progression cost; protection substitutes for every other investment; tiers gate by number alone; upgrades erase older niches; squads bypass costs; safe farming skips the threats that price rewards; or wealth simultaneously improves survival, earning and replacement speed.

A dominant strategy wins across common objectives after time, risk and replacement, not merely within one niche. Examine marginal capability, repeatability and survival against added loss, resource, weight and signature costs. Show assumptions, sensitivity and where the preferred kit changes; never add quantities with incompatible units into a fake value formula.

Return the strongest dominance evidence, surviving niches and the smallest candidate repair with its cost. Missing rates limit numeric confidence without invalidating independent structural findings.
