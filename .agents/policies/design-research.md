# Causal design research

Use for explanations and design transfer, not every edit. Begin with a bounded question and the observed problem or desired effect. Read [canon-ownership.md](canon-ownership.md) for internal evidence.

Keep five claims separate: designer intent, actual implementation, player behavior, observed outcome, and causal inference. An interview establishes what a designer intended; it does not prove player outcomes. A community complaint establishes an observation in a particular cohort, not its prevalence or cause.

Observed Outcome != Claimed Cause != Actual Mechanic != Context that makes the mechanic work.

## Investigation

Follow the relevant causal chain, without turning each item into a compulsory section:

Design Question -> Observed Problem / Desired Effect -> Genre-Level Pattern -> Comparable Implementations -> Mechanism Producing the Result -> Alternative Explanations / Confounders -> Transferability to Eldrain -> First-Order Effects -> Second-Order Effects -> Failure Modes -> Evidence Confidence -> Recommendation / Experiment.

Use four scales where they change the answer:

1. Mechanic: available action, price, information, timing and immediate payoff.
2. System: state transitions, coupled resources, feedback loops, substitution and constraints.
3. Player Ecology: solo/squad, newcomer/veteran, cooperation, predation, population and accumulated capital.
4. Genre / Long-Term Lifecycle: solved play, wiki knowledge, Discord coordination, optimisation, selection effects, saturation and incentives after tens or hundreds of hours.

First-order effects follow directly from the changed rule. Second-order effects follow when players adapt, coordinate, accumulate advantage or change which activities they choose. Trace the steps and conditions, including counterforces and who bears the cost. Separate implementation defects from behavior that remains rational under a correct implementation.

## Evidence

Start with Eldrain internal evidence (owners, explicit decisions, actual playtest data). Then use relevant project references in `10_Reference` or other contextual materials, preserving provenance and dates. Use external evidence when internal material cannot support the claim, a comparison needs verification, or the user requests current research. Obey the host's browsing requirements for current or uncertain external facts. Pure analysis of supplied canon does not require browsing.

For comparisons record game/version/mode/cohort, actual mechanic, reported outcome and evidence source separately. Patch notes and official descriptions support implementation claims; observation, telemetry or controlled tests are needed for behavioral effects. Check confounders such as matchmaking, wipe age, population, economy, map density, communication, input/aim model and survivorship bias. Avoid importing a developer's marketing explanation as a causal finding.

Attach confidence to each consequential claim:

- KNOWN: directly supported at the stated scope by an owner or verifiable observation; an observed result alone does not make its cause known.
- STRONGLY INFERRED: converging evidence or explicit derivation with plausible alternatives checked.
- PLAUSIBLE: coherent mechanism with incomplete discriminating evidence.
- SPECULATIVE: exploratory possibility whose essential assumptions remain untested.

## Transfer and recommendation

Transfer a mechanism under its working conditions, not a game's surface resemblance. Compare its information, cost/loss, population, progression, cadence and UX conditions with Eldrain's owners and Vision. Name what survives, what must change, what conflicts, and what is unknown. A successful mechanic in another game can fail when those conditions change.

Return the causal explanation with evidence, alternatives, immediate and adapted consequences, failure modes and a recommendation. For an empirical unknown, propose the smallest discriminating experiment: competing explanations, controlled variable, observation, affected cohort/time horizon, and what result would change the decision. Do not invent telemetry or numerical confidence. Design intent and acceptable tradeoffs still require design judgment.
