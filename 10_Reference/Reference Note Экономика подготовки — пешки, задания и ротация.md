## 1. Bounded Cast — Causal Case

**[PLAUSIBLE] Recommend a five-Pawn living cast for the first prototype, with six as the immediate comparison.** A bounded cast better supports the proposed account-identity loop than the current broad roster—but only if civic value remains contextual and established advantages can reach a meaningful conclusion. Capacity alone will produce neither attachment nor healthy turnover.

This investigation changes no canon. **KNOWN** identifies documented rules or verified mechanics; **STRONGLY INFERRED** identifies consequences derived from incentives; **PLAUSIBLE** identifies promising but untested designs; **SPECULATIVE** identifies exploratory possibilities.

**The strongest case for a bounded cast**

**[KNOWN]** Current canon explicitly permits a broad library of known specialists and rejects active-slot and reserve restrictions. The proposed cast is a deliberate reversal of that position. [Lifecycle Roster](C:/Hobby/Eldrain/04_Player_Entities/Lifecycle_Roster.md)

**[STRONGLY INFERRED]** With broad retention and negligible holding costs, acquiring another useful specialist usually adds an option without removing one. Over time, civic identity tends toward accumulated coverage: the account increasingly answers opportunities by selecting the appropriate stored person.

Five living slots change the acquisition question:

> “Can this person do something useful?” becomes “Is their contribution worth the person and possibilities I would give up?”

That can produce recognizable accounts. A player might preserve an excellent spear economy, good access to disputed salvage, and strong rescue capability while accepting expensive medicine, weak investigative coverage, and fewer field alternatives.

The benefit is strongest when:

- useful approaches substantially exceed available coverage;
- equipment provides imperfect substitutes for absent people;
- civic advantages change extraction targets and preparation;
- different situations change the relative value of retained Pawns;
- keeping a beloved but imperfect person remains viable.

**The strongest case against it**

**[STRONGLY INFERRED]** A capacity limit restricts the size of the solution; it does not stop the solution being found. With stable opportunities, fixed advantages and known acquisition routes, the community can solve an optimal five.

A bounded cast can also intensify character shopping. Once full, every disappointing admission represents a lost incumbent, acquisition effort, and occupied capacity. Hiding Origin until commitment makes that exchange less informed; it does not necessarily make it more humane.

Broad rosters have genuine advantages:

- unusual field builds can coexist without threatening established relationships;
- players can preserve people without sacrificing ordinary flexibility;
- new characters remain additive discoveries;
- temporary recovery states are less disruptive;
- designers need less machinery to create turnover.

**The field-versus-civic opportunity cost**

**[STRONGLY INFERRED]** The relevant comparison is the civic Pawn against the **marginal fifth field Pawn**, not against the player’s strongest fighter.

Each account deploys one selected Pawn. A party combines independently selected Pawns from separate accounts; it does not deploy one account’s entire cast. Consequently, a fifth field alternative may have modest value if the first four already cover the player’s preferred work. [Party Syndicate](C:/Hobby/Eldrain/08_World_Generation/Hub/Party_Syndicate.md)

Conversely, frequent injuries, recovery cases, strongly differentiated raid conditions or expensive equipment incompatibilities can make that fifth alternative extremely valuable.

**[PLAUSIBLE] Verdict:** bounded cast is the better hypothesis for the requested loop. Broad roster remains the better fallback if testing shows that five slots predominantly create replacement anxiety, narrow experimentation, or the same specialist cabinet at smaller scale.

There is also a limit no tuning can remove: **a stationary, solved economy cannot simultaneously guarantee indefinite character retention and continual economically rational turnover.** Something must change—opportunities, comparative value, civic roles, or actual careers. Some long-term retention should remain a legitimate outcome.

## 2. Civic Identity — Viability Verdict

**[PLAUSIBLE] Identity-based civic value can support substantial economic specialization. Strong local effects are more promising than cosmetic-only consequences for justifying a scarce living slot.**

The key distinction is between an advantage that changes a particular strategy and an advantage through which nearly every profitable action should pass.

### Effects worth pursuing

|Effect|Why it can produce good play|Necessary boundary|
|---|---|---|
|Substantial reduction in a narrow recipe’s material cost|Makes a previously marginal extraction route worthwhile|Specific inputs and outputs; finite exceptional terms or meaningful field dependence|
|Alternative material conversion|Changes which otherwise neglected ingredients matter|Consumes real material; cannot close a profitable recycling loop|
|Choice among authored outcomes|Makes a particular loadout practical without increasing all item power|Limited scope, meaningful sidegrades, no universal best selection|
|Relationship-dependent service terms|Makes lived history economically consequential|Particular obligation or institution; no general reputation multiplier|
|Access to a processing branch or investigation|Opens different preparation and raid intentions|Useful alternatives exist; baseline play remains available|
|Paid institutional rights retained after a completed life chapter|Preserves part of an account strategy through turnover|No per-retiree output, stacking multiplier or free material supply|

**[STRONGLY INFERRED]** “No professions” must constrain effect structure. A Pawn who permanently reduces all trading costs by 30% is functionally a Merchant class, even if the effect is described as scars, charm and personal history.

A narrow spear advantage is healthier because its value depends on the account actually using spears, obtaining their ingredients, accepting their field limitations, and encountering suitable opportunities. But even that can become permanent real estate if its lifetime value is unlimited.

### What does not solve the problem by itself

- **Finite transactions:** one transaction can still accept an arbitrarily large batch.
- **Changing addresses:** several addresses can offer the same economic function.
- **Diminishing returns:** a positive, permanent residual advantage may still justify eternal retention.
- **Raid-sourced ingredients:** another Pawn can gather them while the valuable specialist stays safe.
- **Retirement offers:** offers that can be declined without changing the economics leave the same optimal keeper.
- **Rare eligibility:** rarity can increase the incentive to preserve and farm the specialist.
- **Separate account custody:** squads can still share materials and finished equipment through field transfers.

**[STRONGLY INFERRED]** The strongest protection combines a narrow advantage, bounded total benefit, concrete material consumption and an eventual change in the person’s economic role. Each addresses a different failure.

### Transfer conditions and evidence limits

**[KNOWN]** Eldrain already supports extracted-material recipes, deterministic transaction previews, narrow external services and provenance restrictions. These are useful foundations. Current owners also explicitly reject personal discounts and some proposed Pawn-dependent offers; those restrictions would require revision. [Economy Core](C:/Hobby/Eldrain/06_Economy_Loot/Economy_Core.md), [Barter System](C:/Hobby/Eldrain/06_Economy_Loot/Barter_System.md)

**[KNOWN]** The internal civic references already identify specialist cabinets, safe production and tag shopping as risks. They are reference hypotheses, not behavioral evidence, and their preference against quantitative rewards should not decide this investigation. [Civic Dispatch reference](C:/Hobby/Eldrain/10_Reference/Reference_Notes_Civic_Dispatch.md)

**[PLAUSIBLE]** Healthy transfer requires differences in _current economic use_, not merely more eligibility icons. The player should understand why this person changes this procedure. Personal Tags can supply physical capabilities; relationships and custody can supply social standing. Those facts should retain their own owners rather than being compressed into a universal civic competency score.

## 3. Candidate Civic Value Models

The following three models deserve comparison. **All names, recipes, properties and numbers in the worked examples are invented prototype content, not existing canon.** None uses Race × Spec as a civic class.

### B1 — Strong material terms within a finite institutional program

**[PLAUSIBLE]** A Pawn makes a narrow production strategy substantially cheaper across several paid uses. The exceptional arrangement belongs to a developing institutional situation and eventually concludes.

**Worked spear loop**

1. **Identity:** Mara survived a quench-line collapse and preserved its operating records. A recovered master recognizes her testimony and trusts her reconstruction.
    
2. **Civic opportunity:** drag Mara’s card onto a Stable workshop’s damaged spear-production line.
    
3. **Advantage:** her arrangement permits a reclaimed-wire process for six batches.
    
    An illustrative ordinary recipe requires:
    
    - six spring strips;
    - two resin portions;
    - one ferrule;
    - 12 Rez.
    
    Mara’s version requires three strips, two reclaimed-wire bundles, the same resin and ferrule, and 8 Rez.
    
    At illustrative comparison values of 10 per strip, 3 per wire bundle, 5 per resin, 10 per ferrule and 1 per Rez, the cost changes from **92 to 64: approximately 30% less**.
    
4. **Material decision:** reclaimed wire becomes valuable cargo. The player prioritizes a workshop Anomaly over a route rich in ingredients their account no longer needs as urgently.
    
5. **Gear possibility:** replacing lost spears becomes affordable enough to support a persistent spear-focused playstyle.
    
6. **Next raid:** another retained Pawn carries the spear into a route that rewards its reach, accepting its close-quarters limitations. Mara can also deploy; the economic model does not require her to gather every input.
    
7. **Lifecycle pressure:** successive supplied batches resolve the workshop program. Its exceptional personal terms have a finite remaining value.
    
8. **Turnover/metaprogression:** at completion, Mara can conclude her civic life and leave a narrower processing right, or remain a field Pawn after the special arrangement ends.
    
9. **Future decisions:** the account preserves a viable spear route, but the next rescued person may now justify Mara’s slot through a different relationship or field capability.
    

**Slot cost:** the six illustrative batches save 168 comparison units. That is substantial enough to compare with several raids’ worth of marginal field flexibility, rather than being decorative value.

**Repeatability:** repeatable within the program. A new Pawn, reopened interface, replacement sector or retirement must not reset the same program.

**Long-term optimizer:** rushes useful batches, stockpiles favorable ingredients and considers retirement when remaining personal advantage falls below replacement value.

**Strongest failure:** each new workshop quietly restores the same exceptional terms. Mara then remains an eternal guarantor.

**Disposition:** prototype. Reject the resetting version as **architectural/ecological**. Batch size, total savings and opportunity cadence are **tuning** questions.

### B2 — Finite relationship projects with consequential selection rights

**[PLAUSIBLE]** Identity changes which resolution the player can obtain from a concrete dispute, investigation or Civic Event. The reward can be economically large without becoming a standing percentage.

**Worked selection loop**

1. **Identity:** Iven carried a disputed seal out of an Anomaly and established a relationship with the family whose testimony it supports.
2. **Civic opportunity:** a rare workshop event offers a reconstruction of one authored spear treatment. Iven can provide the missing chain of custody.
3. **Advantage:** instead of accepting the event’s single published branch, the player may choose among three authored treatments, paying the full corresponding materials.
4. **Material decision:** the player chooses corrosion-resistant treatment. Protective resin and intact ceramic fittings become more valuable than the ingredients for the other branches.
5. **Gear possibility:** a spear suitable for repeated work in corrosive conditions becomes practical, with an explicit handling or weight tradeoff.
6. **Next raid:** the account prepares for a flooded industrial Anomaly it previously avoided, carrying the selected treatment and appropriate environmental equipment.
7. **Lifecycle pressure:** Iven’s testimony resolves the dispute and changes the relationship. Repeating the testimony does not reopen the reward.
8. **Turnover/metaprogression:** a later completed personal chapter can preserve the right to commission this treatment under ordinary paid terms, freeing the account from dependence on Iven’s live testimony.
9. **Future decisions:** the player can continue the corrosive-route strategy while retaining or replacing Iven according to his remaining contribution.

**Magnitude:** access to a desired authored outcome can be worth much more than a small discount. Its value is the cost and delay of the alternatives it avoids. “Horizontal” does not mean economically weak.

**Slot cost:** one rare event every twenty hours probably does not justify a permanently occupied slot. The Pawn needs smaller recurring relevance, a currently active project, or useful field participation between exceptional moments.

**Repeatability:** the specific project resolves once. Other projects need genuinely different facts or conflicts, not refreshed rewards for the same witness.

**Long-term optimizer:** learns which relationships open valuable branches and may steer histories deliberately. That is acceptable if several histories support different viable strategies.

**Strongest failure:** an expensive authored-content treadmill. Once projects are solved, the system either loses value or repeats obvious keys.

**Disposition:** strong supporting model, weak sole foundation for an endless macro loop. Content exhaustion is **content-production/ecological**; universally superior selection outcomes are **design/tuning**, depending on whether alternatives possess real tradeoffs.

### B3 — A material advantage earned through the same Pawn’s field work

**[PLAUSIBLE]** A particular lived capability enables a field procedure whose delivered evidence improves a subsequent city transaction.

**Worked filtration loop**

1. **Identity:** Sena has a mutation that lets her detect a specific membrane’s failure response, plus a scar that makes prolonged exposure costly.
2. **Civic opportunity:** a processing address offers a more efficient filtration branch if Sena personally obtains and validates a sample under the relevant field conditions.
3. **Advantage:** the delivered, attested sample supports four filter assemblies from material that ordinarily supports three—a **33% output increase for that batch**, not a general yield bonus.
4. **Material decision:** intact membranes from the named environment become worth carrying; generic scrap does not qualify.
5. **Gear possibility:** the account can afford environmental preparation for more difficult routes.
6. **Next raid:** Sena carries a sampling rig, sacrificing cargo capacity and taking a route that exposes both her useful capability and its cost.
7. **Lifecycle risk:** losing Sena risks a valuable capability. A safe token raid provides no substitute evidence.
8. **Turnover/metaprogression:** after a completed research program, the institution retains a less efficient ordinary version of the process; Sena can retire without erasing the strategy.
9. **Future decisions:** another Pawn can use the paid institutional process, while a future living specialist might improve a different preparation problem.

**Slot cost:** this model values a hybrid most strongly. Sena must survive and perform the work, so poor field capability directly reduces civic value.

**Repeatability:** a new consequential sample or procedure can support another paid batch. Merely entering a new session cannot.

**Long-term optimizer:** finds the safest qualifying route and uses escorts. The real adversary is a coordinated veteran squad, not an unsupported solo player.

**Strongest failure:** civic-asymmetric Pawns become inferior to strong hybrids, and “meaningful participation” becomes a disguised maintenance chore.

**Disposition:** retain as a challenger and occasional content pattern. Do not make it the universal rule for all civic value unless testing demonstrates that intentionally uneven Pawns remain viable.

## 4. Life Closure / Retirement Models

**[STRONGLY INFERRED]** Retirement timing and retirement economics are separate problems. A different trigger cannot repair an unlimited living advantage combined with a flat retirement payout.

### Competing families

|Family|Strongest form|Causal strength|Principal failure|
|---|---|---|---|
|**C1: Deterministic**|Visible personal arc, formed-life decision or final authored job|Player can plan a satisfying ending and compare its value|Wiki solves cheapest retirement; valuable Pawns refuse indefinitely|
|**C2: Emergent**|Changed relationships, completed obligations and a formed civic role make settlement appropriate|Opportunity arises from this life’s actual shape|Players manufacture cheapest qualifying history; rules may feel arbitrary|
|**C3: Probabilistic pressure**|After a protected threshold, qualifying use can directly settle the career|Prevents perfectly scheduled indefinite exploitation when exposure cannot be avoided|Avoidance, threshold parking, perceived confiscation and replacement farming|
|**C4: Hybrid**|State or probability creates an offer; player chooses its consequence|Preserves uncertain timing while allowing meaningful agency|Free rejection preserves the merchant unless economic terms also change|
|**C5: Institutional succession**|Completed civic work ends exceptional personal terms; actual field retirement remains voluntary|Removes permanent economic dependence without requiring the person’s disappearance|Can feel like forced retirement through loss of usefulness; institutions can replace living civic identity|

All five remain hypotheses. C1, C4 and C5 are the strongest initial candidates; C3 should remain a genuine comparator rather than being dismissed for containing randomness.

### Deterministic variants

**[PLAUSIBLE]**

- **Explicit personal arc:** strongest narrative coherence; highest authored burden.
- **Field-earned ending:** can make the final risk meaningful; fails if the objective is merely a qualifying raid.
- **City-earned ending:** supports genuinely civic lives; must consume consequential state or material rather than cheap repetitions.
- **Final authored job:** gives the ending a playable culmination; needs alternatives when an unavailable sector would otherwise strand it.
- **Player-invoked ending after formation:** comparatively cheap to produce and easy to understand; formation cannot mean merely filling three tag slots.

**[KNOWN]** Current Closure requires three reserved and revealed manifestations plus a resolved authored arc, followed by an immediate choice between civic Closure and permanent abandonment of the generic Closure path. That is one specific deterministic design, not a prerequisite for the alternatives. [Life Closure](C:/Hobby/Eldrain/04_Player_Entities/Life_Closure.md)

### Emergent and probabilistic variants

**[PLAUSIBLE]** Emergence should primarily read durable changes: an obligation fulfilled, someone now willing to inherit responsibility, an institution that can continue without the Pawn, or a life that has acquired another plausible future.

Long absence from raids can help contextualize an offer. It is a poor sole progress source: real-time absence rewards waiting or punishes irregular play; counting deployments invites token excursions.

**[STRONGLY INFERRED]** Visible probability supports informed risk-taking but makes optimal stopping easier. Partly hidden probability obscures exact timing, but communities can estimate it, and uncertainty can increase avoidance.

Therefore:

- test **visible direct pressure** as an explicit wager;
- test **partly hidden offer timing** with visible eligibility and a guaranteed eventual offer;
- treat **partly hidden compulsory retirement** as a higher experiential-risk comparator, with a protected minimum tenure and a clear warning before exposure begins.

Hidden timing may improve naturalness. Hidden stakes usually make a scarce economic asset harder to use confidently. That difference requires player testing.

### What declining an offer means

|Decline rule|Consequence|
|---|---|
|Free and repeatable, preserving all old advantages|**Reject as an immortality solution.** It changes no economic incentive.|
|Permanent refusal|Prevents offer fishing, but punishes uncertainty and attachment; may preserve merchant immortality anyway.|
|Currency payment|Becomes affordable upkeep for wealthy keepers and painful upkeep for poorer players.|
|Temporary deferral|Useful if the same offer remains available without logout expiry, reroll or repeated nagging.|
|Transformational decline|Promising: retain field life, conclude the old civic arrangement, and retain only earned institutional rights.|
|Another authored path|Useful when new facts justify it; must not reset the same reward program.|

### Retirement versus raid participation

**[STRONGLY INFERRED]**

- **Full reset:** produces the clearest maintenance loop. Reject.
- **Repeatable partial decay:** changes the required raid frequency; does not remove the loop.
- **Repeatably refreshed threshold delay:** equivalent problem.
- **Finite, nonrenewable postponement:** potentially useful for finishing named work; test whether the extra rule earns its complexity.
- **No interaction:** strongest baseline. Field activity does not erase civic history.
- **Absence increases relevance:** plausible for presentation and offers, without punishment for logging out.
- **Meaningful field participation:** should add evidence or open different work, not wipe old pressure.

A maintenance raid becomes rational when the expected civic loss it avoids exceeds the raid’s time and risk cost. Raising the danger can make the chore harsher without changing its purpose.

### What comparisons actually establish

**[KNOWN]** Gloomhaven’s second-edition FAQ documents personal quests that can lead through scenarios before retirement, and requirements that sometimes distinguish different scenarios or total contribution from repeated actions. This establishes implementable forms of structured qualification—not evidence that they prevent optimization. Its cooperative campaign progression differs materially from Eldrain’s persistent PvPvE economy. [Official FAQ](https://cephalofairgames.github.io/gloomhaven2e-faq/)

**[KNOWN]** Red Hook’s July 2024 FAQ describes DDII Confessions memories that preserve invested heroes and are lost with specified failures. This illustrates how mechanical investment can attach future value to an individual; it does not prove that investment increases deployment rather than preservation. [Developer FAQ](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1940340/discussions/1/3757726797848424320/)

**[KNOWN]** Wildermyth explicitly permits favorite heroes to return in later adventures. That changes the emotional cost of endings compared with Eldrain’s irrecoverable individual lives. Its lifecycle experience should therefore not be transferred without accounting for that difference. [Developer description](https://wildermyth.com/)

## 5. Numbers and Breakpoints

**All values below are illustrative prototype parameters. None is accepted canon.**

### Capacity, coverage and cadence

|Variable|Initial test value|What it is testing|
|---|---|---|
|Living cast|**5**, with **6** sensitivity test|Whether one additional slot dissolves or improves the intended tension|
|Broad-roster comparator|12–15|Whether bounded retention changes decisions rather than just UI load|
|Civic approaches|About 12 across several situations|More useful possibilities than one account can cover|
|Excellent account coverage|2–3 approaches|Recognizable strengths|
|Additional workable coverage|2–3 approaches|Imperfect substitutes without universal coverage|
|Exceptional narrow cost reduction|25–35%|A material advantage capable of paying for a slot|
|Smaller repeatable local advantage|10–20%|Whether modest benefit remains relevant without becoming mandatory|
|Meaningful civic relevance|Roughly once per 2–4 raids for a retained civic specialist|Avoid dead weight between rare events|
|Exceptional material program|4–8 paid batches|Finite total personal advantage|
|Active legacy rights|2|Persistent choice without activating every historical advantage|

**[PLAUSIBLE]** “Twelve approaches” should not become twelve professions. Approaches can overlap across physical capabilities, relationships, evidence and obligations. A single situation should often accept multiple different solutions.

For an acquisition prototype, start with two baseline people and expose enough ordinary rescue/relationship opportunities to approach five over the first dozen returns. This is a pacing test, not a proposed permanent recruitment split.

### Slot break-even

Let:

- \(f\) = relevant transactions per raid;
- \(s\) = saving per relevant transaction;
- \(a\) = amortized value of access and selection;
- \(c\) = extra costs and risk attributable to using the civic capability;
- \(F_5\) = value of the displaced marginal field option.

A simplified civic slot is economically justified when:

\[ f s + a - c > F_5 \]

**[STRONGLY INFERRED]** B1’s illustrative 28-unit saving every second raid contributes 14 units per raid before additional costs.

- Against an 8-unit marginal field alternative, keeping the civic Pawn is attractive.
- Against an 18-unit alternative, the same Pawn needs access value, more frequent use or field usefulness.
- If the saving is valuable across nearly every transaction, the mechanism has probably become too broad.

These are sensitivity examples, not estimates of actual Eldrain raid economics.

### Asymmetric Pawns versus hybrids

**[STRONGLY INFERRED]** If a hybrid has the same civic advantage as an asymmetric Pawn and strictly better field performance, the asymmetric Pawn is dominated. No roster limit changes that.

The design needs actual differences in opportunity:

- the asymmetric Pawn offers better terms in a relevant niche;
- the hybrid covers more situations less efficiently;
- their histories open different projects;
- capabilities compete for limited personal formation opportunities;
- equipment compensates for field weakness at a real cost.

Not every person needs equal expected value. But deliberately asymmetric characters need circumstances in which retaining them is rational.

### A testable offer-hazard curve

After five meaningful qualifying resolutions:

|Resolution|Conditional chance of first offer|Cumulative chance of an offer|
|---|---|---|
|1–5|0%|0%|
|6|10%|10%|
|7|20%|28%|
|8|30%|49.6%|
|9|40%|69.8%|
|10|50%|84.9%|
|11|60%|94.0%|
|12|Guaranteed|100%|

**[KNOWN—calculated]** This curve has mean first-offer timing **8.64 resolutions**, median **9**, and 90th percentile **11**.

Use deterministic resolution 9 as a reasonably matched comparator. Keep rewards and qualification costs equal. The ceiling guarantees an **offer**, not compulsory retirement.

For direct probabilistic retirement, test a separately disclosed version rather than quietly treating the offer curve as consent to removal.

### What counts as meaningful

**[PLAUSIBLE]** Qualification should require a changed situation and a real commitment:

- delivered and consumed material;
- a resolved dispute;
- personally relevant field evidence;
- an obligation that cannot be repeated in the same state;
- a materially different stage of a project.

A candidate threshold might require 8–10 resolutions, at least three kinds of consequential work and at least two persistent relationships or contexts. Count no more than two repetitions of an identical procedure toward formation.

That cap limits repetition; it does not prove meaningfulness. A wiki-known sequence of three trivial task types would still fail.

The count must follow resolved state, not card drags, split transactions, reconnects, repeated dialogue or new session IDs. Hazard must not become cheaper by dividing one meaningful batch into many actions.

### Long-term breakpoints

**[KNOWN—calculated]**

- At a hypothetical 2% chance of a sought identity, 100 independent admissions produce an **86.7%** chance of finding it; 200 produce **98.2%**.
- A Pawn exposed ten times to independent 5% loss risks has about **59.9%** survival; at 10% per exposure, about **34.9%**.
- Three independent accounts covering five of twelve approaches have expected combined coverage of approximately **9.62** approaches. Deliberately complementary accounts can cover all twelve.

**[STRONGLY INFERRED]** These calculations expose different problems: repeated shopping defeats information delay; required exposure can strongly discourage using valuable people; and personal scarcity is weaker than coordinated-group scarcity.

## 6. Closure Metaprogression

**[PLAUSIBLE] The strongest reward is an institutional capability earned through a completed life chapter: meaningful, persistent, materially constrained, and no longer dependent on employing the retired person.**

Examples include:

- commissioning a narrow material treatment;
- using a bounded conversion under ordinary paid terms;
- choosing an authored outcome when supplying a particular rare input;
- retaining access to a particular institution;
- continuing a specific obligation through another Pawn;
- opening deeper Keeper work.

A retired Pawn may remain visible in the city, but there is no assignment slot, production timer, salary collection or per-retiree percentage.

### Why surrendering a rare Pawn can be rational

Compare:

\[ \text{legacy value}+\text{new slot value}+\text{safe ending value} \]

with:

\[ \text{remaining personal civic value}+\text{field value}+\text{attachment to continued play} \]

**[STRONGLY INFERRED]** If the personal civic advantage is an unlimited safe annuity, a bounded retirement reward frequently loses. That is the central reason to conclude exceptional terms.

In B1, after most favorable batches have been used, preserving a narrower spear-processing right and admitting someone with a useful new contribution can be rational—even if Mara’s original combination was exceptionally rare.

Her rarity remains part of a valuable life. It no longer guarantees an infinite future revenue stream.

### Two reward structures worth separating

**Retirement-exclusive vesting — [PLAUSIBLE].** Completing the mature arc and accepting Closure grants the institutional right.

This makes Closure economically consequential, but carries the sharper coercion and burner risks. It needs finite, account-specific projects and no generic payout for another retired body.

**Completed-work vesting — [PLAUSIBLE, preferred].** Completing the civic program earns the right. The concluding choice determines whether the Pawn remains deployable or settles permanently. Either choice ends the exceptional personal arrangement.

This deliberately makes **the work valuable and the ending a real choice**. Closure additionally frees a scarce slot and safely preserves the person’s civic future. It need not pay a death-equivalent bounty to be mechanically meaningful.

The second structure better resists “consume weak people.” It also accepts that a rare Pawn with continuing field and emotional value may remain in the cast.

### Why this does not automatically become a retirement factory

Necessary conditions:

1. **One institutional program, one grant.** New Pawns and endings do not reset it.
2. **No generic reward per life.** There is no broadly fungible retirement XP or permanent percentage.
3. **Qualification comes from consequential work.** Stocked materials alone cannot instantly fabricate a lived relationship.
4. **The account chooses a limited active set of rights.** An archive of discoveries is not simultaneous access to every advantage.
5. **Use consumes materials.** Recipes and permissions do not supply their own inputs.
6. **Ending earlier does not improve the grant.** A terminal flag cannot manufacture unearned evidence or accelerate the next opportunity.
7. **Ordinary departure is available without KIA.** An unsuitable person must not need to die to release capacity.

**[STRONGLY INFERRED]** Active-right limits are insufficient if players can freely switch rights for each transaction. That recreates complete coverage serially. Activation should commit materials or select a meaningful production program, not create a daily cooldown.

There is also an opposite danger: enough institutional rights may make living civic Pawns obsolete. Test established accounts with full archives. If their best strategy becomes five strong fighters plus every useful inherited service, the reward structure has displaced the premise.

### Keepers and burners

**[STRONGLY INFERRED]** The dangerous structure is:

> heterogeneous ongoing value + similar per-person retirement reward + renewable cheap candidates.

Good Pawns have much to lose by retiring. Bad Pawns have little to lose and produce the same permanent reward. Bifurcation follows rationally.

Neither requiring five tasks nor randomizing the offer removes that structure.

The proposed alternative pays for completing a specific program, limits its exceptional advantage, and prevents a new person from restarting its grant. A powerful civic Pawn can therefore be worth bringing to conclusion; a weak replacement cannot recreate the same reward simply by existing.

This is a mitigation to test, not a claim that players will stop comparing people instrumentally.

### Keepers / Anchor continuity

**[KNOWN]** The Anchor text already places direct recognition of the Shard after accumulated roster history. It does not define the gameplay qualification. [The Anchor](C:/Hobby/Eldrain/02_World_Lore/The_Anchor.md)

**[PLAUSIBLE]** Completed lives can supply the strongest evidence of continuity without becoming XP denominations:

- an earlier Pawn made a consequential promise;
- that person died, was lost, settled, or completed another authored chapter;
- a later Pawn encounters the still-existing consequence;
- the account maintains, revises or repudiates the earlier commitment;
- the Keepers can identify a pattern crossing individual identities.

A small quantitative support structure is reasonable. For example, require two coherent chains across at least two Pawn identities, supported by eight qualifying facts from several contexts. Cap repeated evidence from the same event family.

The critical rule is that the evidence was earned through actions **before and after** the ending. KIA does not award additional points.

**Explicit answers:**

- **Only Closure counts?** No. That would privilege the safest farmable ending.
- **Must the account collect different death types?** No. That incentivizes manufactured deaths.
- **Should death and Closure differ?** Yes in available testimony and subsequent problems; not necessarily as different XP values.
- **Can living histories qualify?** They should have an alternative route through completed chapters and transferred responsibilities.
- **Can endings remain central?** Yes. They supply irreversible discontinuities against which the Shard’s continuity becomes legible.
- **Can a visible counter work?** Possibly. Show the supporting cases and missing connections, rather than “retire three more people.”

**[SPECULATIVE]** The late-game payoff could unlock an Archive investigation whose tools and preparation depend on those particular cases. That would make roster history usable gameplay evidence rather than fuel consumed by a progress bar.

## 7. Foundlings and Full-Cast Admission

**[PLAUSIBLE] Prefer city-first rescue settlement, with a one-prospect deferred-admission model as the principal comparator. Reject mandatory immediate replacement as the default.**

**[KNOWN]** Current Foundlings already separate physical rescue from membership and permit civic handoff. They occupy Back Slot, preserve custody history, and conceal exact Origin until irreversible commitment and a subsequent qualifying return. Current canon also accepts declining marginal rescue value for deep rosters. [Shell Foundlings](C:/Hobby/Eldrain/04_Player_Entities/Shell_Foundlings.md)

### D1 — Immediate full-cast replacement before Origin reveal

The player extracts someone, then releases an incumbent before obtaining the newcomer’s exact Origin.

**[STRONGLY INFERRED]**

- Preserves strict capacity.
- Makes an established relationship the price of an unknown mechanical result.
- Encourages rejecting rescues once the cast is strong.
- Still permits delayed inspect-and-discard after admission and reveal.
- Encourages deliberate KIA if death releases a slot more cheaply than ordinary departure.
- Makes retirement farming especially attractive if the outgoing person also pays a reward.

**Verdict:** reject as the default for **architectural and experiential** reasons. A safer return UI or different rarity rate cannot remove the blind-replacement structure.

It can exist as an explicit voluntary decision for a player already wanting to part with an incumbent.

### D2 — One deferred admission prospect

The person reaches safety and receives care. One potential admission remains pending while the player resolves the cast decision.

Necessary limits:

- no deployment or trial raid before admission;
- no civic production or usable specialist capability while pending;
- no exact Origin preview;
- no storage of multiple invitations;
- no real-time expiry;
- another rescue can receive civic settlement even while the invitation remains pending.

**[PLAUSIBLE]** This supports a natural transition when an incumbent is already approaching an ending.

**[STRONGLY INFERRED]** It still provides option value beyond five retained Pawns. It should be described honestly as one bounded admission option, not claimed to be identical to a strict five-person system.

The player can also shop through visible biography and compatibility signals. Preventing exact tag inspection does not prevent all selection.

**Verdict:** prototype. An unlimited or playable intake becomes an **architectural reserve exploit**. Whether one nonplayable prospect improves decisions enough to justify its extra state is an **experiential/tuning** question.

### D3 — Rescue into the city, without automatic Pawn ownership

The rescued person receives a civic destination and may continue as an NPC, witness, family member or participant in a specific unresolved situation.

They do not become a recruit held indefinitely for later activation.

Possible consequences include:

- restoring access to a disrupted address;
- opening an alternative processing investigation;
- resolving a custody dispute;
- creating a finite contract branch;
- retaining a particular relationship or testimony.

**[PLAUSIBLE]** This gives a saturated account reasons to rescue without demanding that every rescued person replace someone.

The central boundary is **no guaranteed recall**. If every saved resident can later be summoned as a Pawn or civic key, the city has become an infinite reserve.

A later voluntary admission can occur through a single authored relationship path. It cannot mean the account owns permanent recruitment rights over all rescued citizens.

**Verdict:** strongest default. Its main risks are **reward ecology** and **content production**, rather than capacity itself.

### Information before commitment

**[PLAUSIBLE]** Use three stages:

1. **Before detach:** physical rescue information—mass, support requirements, immediate danger and route implications.
2. **After reaching safety:** name, broad history, expressed wishes and credible field compatibility; exact Origin remains undisclosed.
3. **After admission and qualifying play:** exact preassigned Origin becomes known.

Do not withhold ordinary human conversation solely because it might inform a choice. Instead test whether visible facts predict valuable Origin combinations so accurately that biography becomes a shopping catalogue.

**[STRONGLY INFERRED]** Hidden Origin is only an information policy. Anti-gacha protection must also reduce the value of endless sampling:

- rare identity is not the only route to important civic strategies;
- desirable histories can develop through play;
- extraordinary personal advantage has a bounded horizon;
- departure grants no refund, bonus roll or accelerated replacement;
- previous project state survives death, handoff and admission;
- the free continuity path supplies no rare starting identity.

### Full cast plus a genuinely valuable newcomer

The recommended sequence is:

> Rescue succeeds → person reaches safety → choose civic settlement or one pending invitation → compare admission with an ordinary departure or already-earned Closure → reveal Origin only after actual commitment.

Rescue must not create Closure eligibility for an incumbent. It can make an existing ending newly relevant; it cannot turn someone into cheap retirement fuel.

If ordinary departure is allowed, it is not a second name for rewarded Life Closure. It frees capacity safely but grants no legacy payout. KIA must not be a cheaper mechanical way to obtain that same space.

### Why continue rescuing with an excellent cast?

**[PLAUSIBLE]** Because some rescues change situations that matter to the current account, rather than because every rescue contains a potentially superior person.

For example:

> A spear-focused account rescues the last available witness to a workshop’s material claim. The person joins the city, the dispute becomes resolvable, and the player can pursue a paid processing branch without replacing any Pawn.

That reward belongs to resolving the particular problem. It must not become a repeatable bounty for every rescued body.

The total economic value includes downstream contracts and access—not just the immediate handoff reward. Otherwise a nominally modest rescue payout can hide the best farming route in the game.

**[STRONGLY INFERRED]** Routine rescue frequency can legitimately decline at cast saturation. Requiring equal value from every capsule forever would push toward power creep, repeatable bounties or compulsory moral penalties.

### Continuity after collapse

**[KNOWN]** The current stable continuity profile already prevents repeated deaths from rolling new mechanical profiles within an epoch, and Welfare equipment cannot become transferable salvage. Those are useful foundations. [Spawn Logic](C:/Hobby/Eldrain/04_Player_Entities/Spawn_Logic.md)

**[PLAUSIBLE]** Preserve and strengthen the following:

- immediate free baseline character when the cast has terminally collapsed;
- no waiting, debt, qualification raid or rare candidate roll;
- fixed mechanical profile;
- no rare civic prehistory or immediate lucrative personal program;
- no epoch or recruitment advance caused by wiping;
- viable baseline equipment when needed;
- no liquidation, duplicate equipment or metaprogression reward from repeatedly using the fallback.

The baseline person can develop through genuine future play. Their arrival should not deliver a rare build.

There is an additional boundary: current admission is blocked while recovery, care or pending admission remains unresolved. Your broader “no playable Pawn” promise also needs an immediate answer when all five are unavailable.

**[PLAUSIBLE]** Prototype one fixed relief presence in that case, without a permanent sixth recruit, civic production or paid Closure. It restores play while leaving other people’s fates unresolved. If an immediate care resolution can satisfy the promise without contradicting recovery rules, that is simpler.

Crucially, make relief available for unavailability itself. Requiring players to kill or abandon everyone first would make deliberate wipe the gateway to assistance.

## 9. Discriminating Prototypes

**[PLAUSIBLE] Build a small economy-and-roster simulation first, then test the surviving loops with players.** Arithmetic can reject impossible incentives; it cannot establish attachment, perceived coercion or whether a raid feels like a chore.

The [full adversarial ledger](C:/Users/Andrii/.codex/visualizations/2026/09/07/01a07c2b-8f74-7d91-8184-825bc73a3eda/bounded-cast-adversarial-ledger.md) records **all 28 requested attacks against 14 candidate/supporting mechanisms**, followed by retirement variants, raid interactions, reward variants and surviving combinations. Its 392 evaluations are analytical, not reported playtest outcomes.

### Prototype 1 — Does bounded retention create identity?

**Hypotheses:** five slots create meaningful account specialization; alternatively they create a solved cabinet or suppress experimentation.

**Minimum scope:** twelve distinguishable Pawns, twelve civic approaches, six raid situations, three material strategies, recovery interruptions and a shared acquisition sequence. Compare five, six and broad retention using identical content.

Run optimized policies across at least 100–200 decisions, then expose players to repeated conditions with new and established account states.

**Observe:** retained composition, reasons for replacement, uncovered needs, repeated use of one Pawn, abandoned experiments, and whether players can describe their account’s strengths and sacrifices.

**Falsification:**

- Reject the bounded-cast hypothesis if five slots consistently produce the same retained composition or mostly cause avoided rescue and experimentation.
- Reject broad roster as the better identity model if it reliably removes meaningful tradeoffs and reduces choices to obvious specialist selection.
- Prefer six over five if it substantially improves attachment and experimentation while preserving incomplete coverage.

**Architecture:** a reserve or freely recallable city population invalidates the bounded condition.  
**Numbers:** capacity, coverage overlap, recovery frequency and acquisition cadence.

### Prototype 2 — Civic specialist, dead weight or Merchant class?

**Hypotheses:** B1/B2 support meaningful asymmetric Pawns; B3 produces healthier risk coupling; alternatively either benefits are negligible or hybrids/merchants dominate.

**Minimum scope:** one complete worked loop for each civic model, three competing equipment strategies, weak-field civic specialists, strong hybrids and strong field specialists.

Sweep local advantage through approximately 10%, 20%, 30% and 40%, with different relevance frequencies and total program budgets.

**Observe:** which Pawn loses the fifth slot, changes in extracted ingredients, gear use, raid targets, total account savings and universal use of a single civic Pawn.

**Falsification:**

- Reject a civic model if it affects transaction choice but never changes extraction or preparation.
- Reject B1 if every attractive setting produces permanent parking and universal economic routing.
- Reject B2 as a core model if its frequency cannot justify a slot without exceptional one-off jackpots.
- Reject B3 as a universal rule if strong hybrids dominate and asymmetric Pawns become irrational choices.
- Reject an apparent healthy setting if it works only before players learn the recipes.

**Architecture:** scope and reset topology.  
**Numbers:** magnitude, frequency, lifetime benefit and marginal field-slot value.

### Prototype 3 — Deterministic versus probabilistic Closure

**Hypotheses:** deterministic planning creates better endings; uncertain timing makes lives feel less scheduled; direct pressure creates useful risk rather than avoidance.

**Minimum scope:** the same mature Pawns, projects and reward values under:

- deterministic offer at resolution nine;
- visible probabilistic offer with the illustrated ceiling;
- partly hidden timing with the same disclosed eligibility and ceiling;
- a clearly disclosed direct-retirement comparator.

Keep offer generation distinct from acceptance and test refusal rules separately.

**Observe:** threshold parking, use avoidance, cheapest-action farming, understanding of stakes, regret, attachment, and whether outcomes feel earned.

**Falsification:**

- Reject deterministic superiority if its timing predictably becomes a checklist while stochastic offers maintain understandable causality and better engagement.
- Reject probabilistic offers if uncertainty suppresses use without improving the experience.
- Reject partly hidden timing if it mainly causes mistrust or reliance on external probability tables.
- Reject direct retirement if valuable Pawns are avoided rather than meaningfully risked, or players report the system taking away the person they were encouraged to value.

**Architecture:** whether the event removes agency and whether exposure can be evaded.  
**Numbers:** minimum tenure, hazard, ceiling and reward timing.

### Prototype 4 — Legacy value, immortality and burner farming

**Hypotheses:** institutional rights enable desirable endings; alternatively they reward rapid turnover or eventually replace living civic identity.

**Minimum scope:** two mature programs and four legacy rights. Compare:

1. cosmetic-only Closure;
2. retirement-exclusive mechanical vesting;
3. completed-work vesting with optional field continuation;
4. a deliberately unsafe per-retirement reward control.

Include cheap recruits, exceptionally useful Pawns, duplicate programs, full archives and wealthy accounts.

**Observe:** time to first retirement, repeated cheapest endings, retention by Pawn quality, living civic participation after legacy accumulation, and the opening sequence chosen by informed optimizers.

**Falsification:**

- Reject cosmetic-only as the turnover mechanism if rare valuable Pawns are never voluntarily surrendered.
- Reject retirement-exclusive vesting if it creates a stable weak-Pawn production line or makes “retire several people first” the optimal opening.
- Reject completed-work vesting if there is no meaningful reason to choose Closure even after personal advantage ends, unless enduring retention is accepted as the intended outcome.
- Reject institutional rights if mature accounts stop valuing living civic participants or stop extracting the materials those rights were meant to organize.

**Architecture:** per-person payouts, duplicated grants, passive workers and unrestricted active archives.  
**Numbers:** remaining personal value, right strength, activation cost and program length.

### Prototype 5 — Full-cast rescue without human gacha

**Hypotheses:** D2 preserves humane choice; D3 keeps rescue valuable without roster ownership; immediate replacement provides stronger but acceptable stakes.

**Minimum scope:** a full cast containing one beloved weak Pawn, one rare civic specialist and three strong field options; several rescue situations with identical hidden Origin distributions.

Compare D1, D2 and D3. Include exact post-rescue Origin preview as an information control, not an assumed improvement.

**Observe:** pickup decisions, preexisting desire for turnover, admissions before and after reveal, safe departures, deliberate deaths, repeated capsule targeting, and value assigned to civic settlement.

**Falsification:**

- Reject D1 if players avoid rescue primarily because it demands an uninformed sacrifice.
- Reject D2 if the pending person becomes a free trial, a permanent extra specialist, or a shopping queue.
- Reject D3 if saturated players find its consequences irrelevant—or if its rewards make routine capsule farming the dominant economy.
- Reject delayed reveal as sufficient anti-gacha protection if it merely adds one cheap raid before discarding a disappointing Pawn.
- Reject any model where killing is mechanically preferable to an ordinary safe departure.

**Architecture:** admission versus custody, recall rights, safe departure and prospect usability.  
**Numbers:** information predictiveness, rescue cost, downstream reward value and admission frequency.

### Prototype 6 — Veteran, squad and continuity attack

**Hypotheses:** surviving packages preserve extraction, personal scarcity and the right to play after long optimization.

**Minimum scope:** three coordinated accounts; known recipes and tag distributions; mature trader networks; large starting stockpiles; two active legacy rights each; strongest available casts; terminal collapse and all-unavailable states.

Test at least these packages:

- **B1 + deterministic Closure + bounded legacy + deferred admission**;
- **B1/B2 + hybrid offer/civic succession + city-first rescue**;
- **B3 + deterministic or hybrid Closure + deferred admission**.

Do not enumerate every combination. Focus on the interactions that change the incentives.

**Observe:**

- pooled production and transferred equipment;
- convergence on one profitable route;
- whether escorts trivialize same-Pawn field qualification;
- how quickly old materials replace fresh extraction;
- whether the best five remain stationary after opportunities are solved;
- deliberate wipe attempts;
- continuity profile, equipment and reward duplication;
- progression per completed objective versus progression per ended life.

**Falsification:**

- Reject a package if ordinary squad cooperation removes its intended material constraints.
- Reject finite-program protection if changing Pawns or account state refreshes its total benefit.
- Reject same-Pawn risk coupling if escorted qualification becomes routine upkeep.
- Reject late-game evidence if churn increases progression without additional consequential work.
- Reject continuity if wiping improves character quality, produces transferable value, or becomes necessary to escape an unavailable cast.
- Reject claims of continual turnover if the saturated simulation has no reason for it. Either accept stable retention or redesign the underlying lifecycle.

**Architecture:** reward conservation, personal entitlement boundaries, fixed fallback and terminal-event attribution.  
**Numbers:** transferable subsidy, stockpile depletion, squad efficiency and relative activity returns.

**[PLAUSIBLE] Initial recommendation:** prototype **five living Pawns**, **strong narrow material advantages**, **finite institutional programs**, **two active legacy rights**, and **city-first rescue**. Compare a deterministic Closure offer with a probabilistic offer under matched economics. Keep direct retirement pressure and same-Pawn field qualification as serious challengers.

The decision to advance should depend on one observable result: **players use identity to choose what to extract and how to prepare, bring valuable civic lives to meaningful conclusions, and continue rescuing people without organizing the cast into immortal profit assets and disposable progression fuel.**
